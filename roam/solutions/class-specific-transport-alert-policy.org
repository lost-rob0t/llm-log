#+title: Class-specific transport alert decision stream
#+filetags: :llm-log:solution:transport:alerts:policy:

* Solves
[[id:llm-log-transport-alert-policy-gap][Typed transport errors lacked class-specific alert decisions]]

* Approach
Represent alerting as a safe, append-only decision projection instead of embedding a notification transport in the proxy. =AlertPolicyRule= maps typed transport evidence to an action, severity, recovery grace, rate class, rate window, and maximum notification allowance. =AlertPolicyActor= serializes those projections into =alert-decisions.jsonl=.

The policy is intentionally class-specific:
- downstream/client disconnect: =log_only=, =info=, no notification allowance;
- account/gateway failure: immediate =alert=, =warning=, =account_gateway=, 900-second rate window, one notification allowance;
- transient provider/router failure: =alert_if_unrecovered=, =warning=, ten-second retry grace, 60-second rate window;
- stream protocol/integrity failure: =alert_if_unrecovered= with the same ten-second grace but a distinct =protocol_integrity= class and 300-second rate window;
- unknown transport failure: immediate warning-level alert policy with a bounded 60-second rate window.

The existing =CorrelationActor= owns the live provider-error/recovery policy bridge. When it receives a typed error it sends the safe error projection to =AlertPolicyActor= without allowing policy failure to poison the correlation actor. When retry correlation later produces a recovery, it emits a second =suppress_recovered= decision linked to the original failed event. This gives a future dispatcher enough evidence to wait for recovery grace and cancel a pending notification without teaching the proxy how to send mail, Discord messages, webhooks, or pages.

The decision projection deliberately omits =request_sha256= and routing-attempt provider details. Account/gateway errors inherit the earlier model-attribution safeguard and keep =model= absent.

* Boundary
This is a policy/decision stream, not a notification dispatcher. The fields describe when and how aggressively a future notifier may act; this code does not contact any external notification service. Model/structured-output anomaly classification remains separate from the transport domain.
